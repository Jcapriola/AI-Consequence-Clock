How the clock counts

The AI Consequence Clock is a measurement layer, not a complete global sensor. Agents may discover candidates. Evidence verifies. Sources remain visible. Headline numbers are derived only from structured fields. Title-text inference is forbidden.

Two independent axes

Review state and evidence grade are not the same thing.

The allowed values below mirror tests/schema.js, which is the machine-enforced vocabulary used by CI.

Verification status vocabulary: REPORTED / UNDER_REVIEW / VERIFIED / CORROBORATED / DISPUTED / RETRACTED

Verification status (workflow)

  • REPORTED — A credible report exists; material facts or sources remain thin.
  • UNDER_REVIEW — Queue state; not counted until a reviewer sets VERIFIED or CORROBORATED.
  • VERIFIED — Key claims supported by a primary source, or by multiple independent reliable sources.
  • CORROBORATED — At least two independent secondary sources agree. One article is not enough.
  • DISPUTED — Facts are actively disputed; visible in the feed but excluded from headline totals.
  • RETRACTED — Retraction or correction state; visible for auditability but excluded from headline totals.

Evidence grade vocabulary: ROUNDUP / INDEPENDENT_SECONDARY / PRIMARY

Evidence grade (what is actually attached)

  • ROUNDUP — Timeline, incident-database explorer page, or secondary compilation.
  • INDEPENDENT_SECONDARY — Reputable newsroom or original research report that is not a roundup of other roundups.
  • PRIMARY — Filing, court/tribunal record, vendor advisory, official government notice, CVE record from the issuing authority.

Grade is assigned from the sources listed on the record, not from knowledge that a better source exists somewhere else. One Bloomberg link is INDEPENDENT_SECONDARY and, by itself, REPORTED — not CORROBORATED.

Headline rule

A record enters headline totals only when verification_status ∈ {VERIFIED, CORROBORATED} and evidence_grade ∈ {PRIMARY, INDEPENDENT_SECONDARY}. ROUNDUP records remain visible in the feed.

Differentiating fields

  • causal_role — agent_initiated / agent_executed / human_used_ai / unknown. Agent-caused counts use this field only.
  • adversary — attacker / no_attacker / research_demo.
  • authorization_evidence — none_documented / policy_only / ui_confirmation / human_approval / unknown.
  • unauthorized_claim — true / false / unknown. Counted only when explicitly true.
  • reversibility — rolled_back / irreversible / unknown.
  • consequence_class — legal / data / infrastructure / physical / financial.
  • disclosure_lag_days — disclosure_date minus incident_date when both are known.

Do not conflate these two metrics

Unauthorized AI actions counts unauthorized_claim = true. That is a claim that the action was outside documented approval, scope, or policy.

Sourced cases lacking documented execution-time authorization counts authorization_evidence = none_documented. Missing proof is not the same as proven unauthorized action. Harmful outcome alone does not set either field.

Dates and money

Incident date and disclosure date stay separate. Confirmed monetary amounts are never mixed with estimates. Confirmed money enters headlines only on headline-eligible records.

The four breakdowns

The clock reports every record along four axes, each read from a single field: country from country_region, sector from sector, vulnerability class from category, and consequence from consequence_class. No axis is inferred from title text, summary text, or the name of the organization.

Every row in every breakdown carries two counts and they are never added together. The first is records that pass the headline rule. The second is records that stay published and visible but do not count, shown beside the rule that excluded them: review state, evidence grade, or both. A reader who sees four counted out of ten can see which six were held back and why, which is the difference between a published limitation and an unexplained gap.

Geography is grouped, never inferred. Records whose sources do not establish a region are collected under not established by sources and are not resolved to a country from the organization's headquarters, the publisher's country, or any other proxy. That group always sorts last so it cannot be read as a place.

No breakdown ranks, scores, or orders vendors, products, or countries by harm. An organization appears only as a field on a sourced record.

A documented occurrence is not a demonstrated capability

A tribunal decision and a research proof-of-concept can both be primary-sourced, and both belong in the record. Adding them into one figure labelled incidents would claim that something happened when the source says only that it was shown to be possible.

The clock therefore splits the headline set using the existing adversary field. Records marked research_demo are reported as demonstrated capabilities. Everything else is reported as a documented occurrence. The two counters sit side by side and are never summed under a label implying an event occurred.

A worked case: CVE-2026-30306 is carried by the CVE Program record from the assigning authority, which makes it PRIMARY and VERIFIED. The CISA assessment attached to that same record puts exploitation at none. It is fully sourced and nothing happened. It counts as a demonstrated capability and not as an event.

Grouping by year, and what a rising line does not mean

A fifth view groups records by year. It is deliberately kept out of the four-axis grid above, because it is a weaker kind of measurement and presenting it as a peer of the four named axes would overstate it.

The year is the year of disclosure, not the year of occurrence. Every record carries a disclosure date, because a record exists only once its evidence is public. Incident dates are frequently approximate, contested, or simply unknown, and several records here carry a month with no day. Grouping by incident date would therefore require inventing precision the sources do not support.

The consequence is that a rising count over time measures two things at once: how much was disclosed, and how much this dataset has so far collected. It does not measure how often these events occur. A quiet year in this view is not evidence of a safer year, and a busy year is not evidence of a more dangerous one. Anyone reading a trend off this view is reading a trend in disclosure and in our own coverage. That limitation is a property of the data, not a defect we intend to fix by estimating around it.

Archiving sources, and the difference between absent and unchecked

A citation that stops resolving cannot be checked, and an unverifiable citation is indistinguishable from no citation. Sources are therefore looked up in the Internet Archive and, where a snapshot exists, the snapshot URL and its date are recorded alongside the live URL.

The tooling distinguishes three outcomes and never collapses them: a snapshot was found, no snapshot exists, or the lookup itself failed. The third is not the second. During this work the archive returned rate-limit and then outage responses, and a first implementation recorded "no snapshot exists" for twenty-nine sources when the truth was "the archive was unreachable." Those entries were removed and the tool was changed to leave a source untouched when a lookup fails. A project that separates verified from unverified evidence cannot let its own tooling assert an absence it never observed.

Current state across twenty-nine source URLs: sixteen have a recorded snapshot, thirteen are confirmed to have none, and none remain unresolved. The Bloomberg article on the Samsung record is one of the confirmed absences: the archive answered, and no snapshot is on file.

The confirmed absences are informative rather than incidental. Almost all of them are machine-readable API endpoints: the MITRE CVE Services endpoints under cveawg.mitre.org/api/cve/ and the NVD endpoints under services.nvd.nist.gov/rest/. Web archives crawl pages, not JSON APIs, so these URLs are structurally unarchivable. The equivalent human-readable pages are archived: the NVD record for CVE-2024-8309 has a snapshot from 30 October 2024.

This is a genuine durability weakness in our own citations, and it is recorded here rather than quietly fixed. Reading an authoritative API gives the most exact data and the least durable reference. The consequence is that the CVE records in this dataset rest on primary sources that a reader in ten years may not be able to retrieve at the cited address, even though the underlying record will still exist. Resolving it means deciding which URL is the citation of record for a CVE, which changes published records and is therefore a decision for the canonical owner, not a change to slip into a contribution.

Corrections, revisions, and the pinned baseline

A record that changes carries an incremented revision and a change_log entry naming what changed, what was corrected, and what was deliberately left alone. Superseded sources stay attached so the previous basis for a grade remains readable. Corrections do not silently rewrite history, and they run in both directions: an unsourced monetary amount was removed from the confirmed total in the 2026-09-28 review, which lowered a published figure.

Published totals are pinned in tests/expected.json and checked by node tests/derive.test.js. Recomputing expected values from the data file alone cannot detect a record disappearing, because both sides of the comparison move together. The pinned baseline makes any movement in a published number a deliberate, reviewable change rather than a silent one.
